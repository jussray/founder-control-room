# Johnstown AI Center — City Hall Working Meeting Packet

Status: meeting-ready internal execution packet

This packet is designed for one City Hall working session. It does not imply City endorsement, sponsorship, pilot approval, financing approval, site approval, or any other external commitment.

## Meeting purpose

Turn the current Johnstown AI Center plan into a defined City-side decision path for a small, reversible 90-day pilot before any permanent-facility commitment.

## The pilot in one paragraph

Johnstown AI Center is proposed as a practical AI workforce, small-business implementation, and entrepreneurship pilot serving Greater Johnstown and Cambria County. Stage 1 runs for 90 days using partner or rented commercial space and portable equipment. It tests real demand, measurable participant outcomes, and an earned-revenue model before any dedicated-building decision.

## Operator

Founder / project lead: Raylene McGill.

Launch model: founder/operator at launch, with part-time or contract support added only after paid demand, repeatability, or documented workload proves the need.

## Who the 90-day pilot is for

- Local small businesses that need practical AI diagnostics, implementation, training, or ongoing support.
- Residents who want practical AI and digital-workforce skills.
- Aspiring founders who need to move from an idea to a testable offer or prototype.

## Existing success metrics

The current Stage 1 plan defines success as:

1. 10 local businesses participate in diagnostics, implementation, or training.
2. 20 or more residents participate in practical AI / digital-workforce training.
3. 5 founder projects complete an idea-to-testable-offer or prototype cycle.
4. At least $12,500 in earned or contracted revenue is produced during the first 90 days.
5. At least 3 recurring support clients or repeat business customers are secured.
6. One partner-space model is proven before a permanent-facility decision.

Attendance alone does not count as commercial validation. Workshop participation, paid conversion, repeat business, and recurring support remain separate measures.

## Evidence to collect during the pilot

- participant and business counts without publishing unnecessary personal information;
- signed or otherwise verifiable paid/contracted work where applicable;
- before/after operational measures when a business agrees to measure them;
- repeat or recurring customer evidence;
- partner-space use and scheduling evidence;
- delivery cost and capacity observations;
- written customer or partner confirmation for material outcome claims;
- explicit UNKNOWN status where a result cannot be verified.

No fingerprint, proof cookie, receipt, attendance record, or repository state grants external authority. These are evidence/state markers only.

## Stage 1 space model

- Partner or rented commercial space.
- Portable/shared devices, networking, and AV/mobile-lab equipment.
- No Stage 1 real-estate purchase.
- A dedicated lease or facility is evaluated only after sustained utilization, recurring revenue, partner support, positive cash-flow evidence, and a quantified space constraint.

## Current financing posture

The existing plan uses **$25,000 as a planning case**, not as a claim that the City has approved a $25,000 loan.

Before a formal City Job Development Loan application is treated as ready, the project still needs to reconcile the final package with current City program requirements, including:

- an eligible private non-residential City location and acceptable site-control evidence;
- the applicable financing-share cap and resulting total project cost;
- required owner equity;
- remaining private or other financing commitments;
- the required job-creation treatment for the requested City loan amount;
- current major facility, equipment, insurance, and setup quotes; and
- confirmation from the City that the final structure fits the applicable loan component.

If verified quotes or City underwriting requirements materially change the use-of-funds assumptions, the financing plan should be revised before any formal application. The project should not inflate the request merely to fit a financing program.

## Explicit City Hall asks for this working session

The meeting should end with answers, owners, or written next steps for these five asks:

1. **City-side owner:** Who, if anyone, owns the next City decision or coordination step for this initiative?
2. **City role:** Does the City want to sponsor, host, refer, finance, connect, or simply review the pilot? A review-only answer is valid and should be recorded accurately.
3. **Pilot evidence:** What exact evidence would City economic-development staff want to see before supporting a larger phase or facility discussion?
4. **Funding + site path:** What Job Development Loan component, site-control form, job-creation treatment, owner-equity requirement, and financing mix would apply to this Stage 1 model?
5. **Next submission:** What exact document, application, partner proof, facility proof, or committee step should happen next, who receives it, and on what timetable?

## Decisions the meeting does not need to make

The session does **not** need to approve:

- a permanent building;
- a long-term lease;
- a larger capital raise;
- City endorsement;
- a City loan;
- a grant application;
- hiring beyond verified demand;
- public claims of partnership.

Those remain separate decisions with separate evidence and authority.

## Stop / rollback conditions

The Stage 1 model stays deliberately reversible:

- If business demand is weak, pause expansion and reduce fixed cost.
- If workshops attract people but do not convert to paid or sponsor-funded next steps, narrow to the strongest commercial service.
- If partner space falls through, use an alternate eligible partner/rental location rather than forcing a permanent facility.
- If current quotes break the $25,000 planning case, revise the financing model before applying rather than hiding the gap.
- If City participation remains review-only, continue validating demand through non-City partners rather than representing City sponsorship that does not exist.

## Required written meeting output

A successful working meeting should produce a short written receipt containing:

- meeting date and participants;
- the City-side owner or an explicit statement that no City owner is assigned;
- the City role, if any;
- the next City decision/submission path;
- the applicable site-control and financing requirements;
- any requested pilot evidence or revisions;
- the next action, owner, and target date.

Until that receipt exists, the City Hall working-meeting gate remains OPEN.
