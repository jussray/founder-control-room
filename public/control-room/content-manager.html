What can go public

Only claims covered by verified evidence and marked public-safe.

  • What changed
  • What was learned
  • Why it matters
  • Optional public proof link

What stays behind the curtain

The sauce guard withholds material that creates unnecessary copying, privacy, or security risk.

  • Private implementation and prompts
  • Raw diffs and credentials
  • Private metrics and unreleased roadmap
  • Customer and security-sensitive details

Who is allowed to decide

Chief suggests. Analytics observes. Current You authorizes. FCR binds the exact packet to the review window.

  • FutureYou is advisory only
  • Missing metrics stay UNKNOWN; analytics can improve later drafts, never authorize them
  • Edits after approval invalidate the authorization
  • Share-now is forbidden for this lane
Current execution boundary: the governed founder-progress packet and review-window bridge are implemented. Live provider writes remain a separate server-side authorization and credential gate, so this page does not pretend a post was scheduled or published.

Output becomes the next gate

Proof → draft → review → approval → schedule → publish → metrics

A successful step never implies the next one succeeded. Each provider artifact must be read back before the conveyor advances.

  1. 1

    Verified proof

    Bind the content request to an authoritative repository, exact commit, rendered evidence, and truthful claim boundary.

    FCR exact-head receipt
  2. 2

    Create draft

    Create reviewable copy and optional media. This step must not publish or silently approve the post.

    create_linkedin_post
  3. 3

    Review

    Inspect final text, media count, target account, proof links, and any claim that could outrun the evidence.

    add_post_comment
  4. 4

    Founder approval

    Approval moves an unscheduled item to approved or a scheduled item to scheduled. It does not publish immediately.

    approve_post
  5. 5

    Schedule

    Set or change an intentional date and time. Scheduling remains reversible before the provider publishes.

    reschedule_post / bulk_schedule_posts
  6. 6

    Explicit publish

    Immediate publication belongs to the generic content lifecycle under its own explicit authority. The first-party founder-progress lane above remains review-window only.

    publish_now(confirmPublication: true)
  7. 7

    Metrics receipt

    Read back external status, logs, and available engagement metrics, then retain the receipt in FCR without turning performance into publish authority.

    get_post_status / sync_post_metrics

Founder Control Room owns

Authority and evidence

  • Exact source repository, branch, commit, and rendered proof.
  • Draft claim boundaries and review notes.
  • Current You approval and scheduling intent.
  • Final provider receipt, rollback path, and next gate.

Provider actuator owns

External platform state

  • External OAuth and target account identity.
  • Post IDs, states, logs, retry queue, and platform errors.
  • Scheduled and published timestamps.
  • Available platform engagement metrics.

Fail closed

A provider limit, authorization error, or failed write is BLOCKED.

FCR must never translate a 402, missing OAuth connection, failed schedule, absent post ID, or missing provider receipt into “approved,” “scheduled,” or “published.”
