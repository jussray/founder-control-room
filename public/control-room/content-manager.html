What can go public

Only claims covered by verified internal evidence, marked public-safe, and valid for their declared time boundary.

  • What changed
  • What was learned
  • Why it matters
  • Public proof link only when it improves the story

What stays behind the curtain

The sauce guard withholds material that creates unnecessary copying, privacy, or security risk.

  • Private implementation and prompts
  • Raw diffs and credentials
  • Private metrics and unreleased roadmap
  • Customer and security-sensitive details

Who is allowed to decide

Chief suggests. Analytics observes. Current You authorizes. FCR binds exact copy, temporal meaning, and execution authority.

  • FutureYou is advisory only
  • Missing metrics stay UNKNOWN; analytics can improve later drafts, never authorize them
  • Edits after approval invalidate the authorization
  • Buffer preflight happens before the one-shot approval is consumed

Founder Control Room owns

Authority and evidence

  • Exact source repository, commit, evidence, and temporal claim meaning.
  • Public-safe draft boundaries and sauce withholding.
  • Fresh Current You approval, one-shot reservation, and schedule authority.
  • Audit trail, provider readback capture, rollback path, and next gate.

Buffer owns

Current delivery transport

  • Transport account authorization and configured channel identity.
  • Schedule acceptance, provider request IDs, transport errors, and ambiguity.
  • Delivery status exposed through provider readback.
  • No authority to change copy, approve a post, or declare unsupported publication truth.

Fail closed

A missing truth gate, Buffer configuration gap, rejected or ambiguous write, or absent readback is BLOCKED or UNKNOWN.

FCR must never translate capability, approval, dispatch, a Buffer schedule request, or missing provider evidence into “published.” Publication requires terminal readback bound to the authorized execution.
